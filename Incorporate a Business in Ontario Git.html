Incorporate a Business in Ontario: A Practical Guide

Incorporating in Ontario creates an independent legal entity under the Business Corporations Act (OBCA). This structure provides liability protection and may offer tax advantages when retaining business profits.

Unlike a sole proprietorship, a corporation owns property, signs leases, and incurs debt independently. Personal assets generally receive protection from business liabilities. However, this shield is not absolute: personal guarantees, unpaid wages, and statutory director obligations can still create personal exposure.

When Should You Incorporate Your Business in Ontario?

You do not need a corporation on day one. A sole proprietorship suffices when starting small, operating with limited liability risk, and drawing business income for personal use.

Once your business generates consistent profits, incorporating becomes viable—especially if you retain earnings inside the company rather than withdrawing everything. Tax advantages depend on your personal tax rate, business income, and small business deduction eligibility. Incorporating also helps when onboarding investors or signing major commercial contracts.

📌 Important GST/HST Registration Note: The CRA's $30,000 small-supplier threshold may require you to register for a GST/HST account. The timing depends on whether you cross the threshold in one quarter or four consecutive quarters.

How to Incorporate in Ontario: 6 Steps

Step 1: Choose Your Ontario Corporation Name

Before filing, choose your corporate name structure:

  • The Numbered Corporation: You can use a numbered corporate name (for example, 12345678 Ontario Inc.) and register a separate business name later if you want to operate under another name.
  • The Named Corporation: Your chosen name must meet Ontario's naming requirements and generally end with a legal identifier (Inc. or Corp.). Professional corporations have additional naming rules.

Step 2: Get a NUANS®Report (If Required)

Named corporations generally require an official Ontario-biased NUANS® report. A preliminary name search can help identify possible conflicts. The report must be dated no more than 90 days before the Articles of Incorporation are submitted. Numbered corporations generally do not require a NUANS® report.

Official source: Ontario Regulation 394/21 – Names and Filings

Step 3: Prepare Your Articles of Incorporation

The Articles of Incorporation set out your company's structure, including share classes and restrictions. Depending on your plans, a share structure may include Class A Voting Common Shares (for founders), Class B Non-Voting Common Shares, and Class C Preferred Shares.

However, issuing shares to family members requires careful tax planning. Canada's Tax on Split Income (TOSI) rules may restrict certain dividend income, even when non-voting shares are used.

Official source: CRA – Tax on Split Income

Step 4: File Through the Ontario Business Registry (OBR)

Once your application is accepted through the Ontario Business Registry (OBR), you receive your Certificate of Incorporation and Ontario Corporation Number (OCN). A unique Company Key manages future corporate filings. Keep it secure and confidential.

Step 5: Set Up Your Corporate Minute Book and Records

After incorporation, complete and maintain your Corporate Minute Book. It typically contains corporate bylaws, director and shareholder resolutions, and share registers.

Maintain a private Individuals with Significant Control (ISC) register identifying anyone owning or controlling 25% or more of corporate voting shares. Update the register annually and within 15 days of changes.

Official source: Ontario – Beneficial Ownership Information Requirements

Step 6: Set Up CRA Accounts and Required Registrations

Upon incorporation, the CRA generally automatically assigns a Business Number (BN) and a Corporate Income Tax (RC) program account.

Depending on your needs, you may require additional accounts such as GST/HST (RT) and Payroll Deductions (RP). If your business requires WSIB coverage, you have 10 calendar days from hiring your first employee to register.

Official sources: CRA – Corporation Income Tax Program Account · WSIB – How to Register

Types of Corporations in Ontario: Specialized Options

Ontario businesses can use different corporate structures depending on their industry, ownership needs, and long-term goals:

  • Personal Real Estate Corporations (PREC): Allow eligible real estate professionals to earn commercial commissions through a corporation, subject to brokerage agreements and RECO regulations.
  • Professional Corporations (PC): Regulated professionals, including physicians, lawyers, and accountants, may incorporate under profession-specific legislation. Incorporation does not remove personal liability for professional negligence.
  • Holding Companies: Commonly used to hold passive investments, real estate, or shares in operating businesses separately to manage operational risk.
  • Ontario Not-for-Profit Corporations (ONCA): Formed under the Not-for-Profit Corporations Act for public benefit or community objectives without share capital. Separate CRA charitable registration is required to issue official donation receipts.

Official sources: RECO – Personal Real Estate Corporations · Ontario – Business Corporations Act · Ontario – Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010 · CRA – Charities and Giving

Common Post-Incorporation Mistakes to Avoid in Ontario

After incorporation, several filing and compliance requirements still apply:

  • Missing the 60-Day Initial Return: Ontario corporations generally must file an Initial Return through the OBR within 60 days of incorporation to confirm director and corporate details.
  • Inaccurate Registered Office Address Your corporation must continuously maintain an accessible physical registered office address in Ontario where legal notices and official documents can be served.
  • Failing to Maintain Corporate Records Banks and commercial lenders frequently request your corporate Minute Book and share registers to verify ownership and corporate standing before opening business accounts.
  • Forgetting the Annual Return Ontario corporations must file an Annual Return through the OBR within six months after their fiscal year-end, which is a separate obligation from the annual CRA T2 corporate tax return.

Official sources: Ontario – Corporations Information Act · Ontario Business Registry

Master Business License in Ontario (2026): Cost, Steps & Renewal

Ontario no longer issues a Master Business Licence (MBL). Since the Ontario Business Registry (OBR) launched on October 19, 2021, new filings receive a Certificate of Registration instead. Existing MBLs remain valid until expiry or cancellation. Sole proprietors, general partnerships, and corporations must register qualifying business names under Ontario’s Business Names Act.

Registering a business name does not create a new legal entity or change the underlying business structure. Sole proprietors and general partners generally remain personally liable for business obligations; a corporation using an operating name remains a separate legal entity.

Official source: Ontario Business Registry – Business Names Act Requirements

Who Needs a Master Business License in Ontario?

Register a business name when operating under a name other than the legal name required for your business structure. The registration records your operating name and may be requested by financial institutions as proof of business registration.

  • Sole Proprietorships: Freelancers and contractors operating under a trade name.
  • General Partnerships: Two or more partners trading under a joint business name.
  • Corporate Operating Names: Corporations (e.g., 12345678 Ontario Inc.) operating a distinct trade brand.
  • New Ventures: Entrepreneurs testing commercial viability with low upfront costs.
📌 CRA Tax & GST/HST Rule: Sole proprietors report business income on personal CRA T1 returns (Form T2125). GST/HST registration is generally required when worldwide taxable supplies, including those of associated persons, exceed $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters.

Official source: CRA – Business Reporting

How to Register a Master Business License in Ontario

Step 1: Conduct a Preliminary Business Name Search

The Ontario Business Registry does not screen for conflicting names. Search CIPO’s Canadian Trademarks Database and consider an Ontario NUANS® search to identify potential conflicts before registering. Using an existing trademark may create infringement risks.

Step 2: Assemble Your Registration Information

Prepare all mandatory statutory details prior to starting the online application:

  • Business Activity (NAICS Code): Select the North American Industry Classification System code accurately representing your commercial operations.
  • Business Address: Provide the principal place of business address. If the business has a location in Ontario, provide its Ontario address; otherwise, provide its principal address outside Ontario. Include the required address for service.
  • Owner Information: Legal full names, residential addresses, and Canadian contact details of the sole proprietor, general partners, or corporate directors.

Step 3: Submit Registration via the Ontario Business Registry (OBR)

Submit your registration directly through the OBR system or through an authorized filing intermediary like AMQLegals. Upon approval, the registry issues your official Master Business License / Business Name Registration certificate along with your 9-digit Business Identification Number (BIN) and a confidential OBR Company Key required for future administrative filings and renewals.

Step 4: Open a Commercial Bank Account & Set Up CRA Accounts

Take your official registration certificate and 9-digit BIN to your chosen financial institution to open a dedicated commercial bank account under your operating trade name. Afterwards, link your registration to the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) by requesting a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN) for GST/HST (RT) if your gross revenue meets the $30,000 statutory threshold, or a Payroll Account (RP) if hiring employees.

Official sources: Ontario.ca – Register Your Business Name Online · CIPO – Canadian Trademarks Database

Common Mistakes After Business Name Registration

Maintaining an active Master Business License in Ontario requires ongoing compliance with provincial registry rules:

  • Missing the 60-Day Renewal Window: Registrations are valid for 5 years and renewable 6 months prior to expiry or up to 60 days past expiry. Exceeding the 60-day post-expiry deadline lapses the registration permanently, forfeits your Business Identification Number (BIN), and causes financial institutions to freeze merchant accounts.
  • Assuming Trademark-Level Name Rights: Registration does not give exclusive name rights. Consider CIPO trademark registration for stronger nationwide protection.
  • Failing to File Amendments within 15 Days: Updates to your registered office address, ownership structure, or commercial activities must be filed in the OBR within 15 calendar days (statutory amendment fee is CAD $0).
  • Neglecting Municipal Trade Permits (BizPaL): An MBL does not replace municipal trade licenses or zoning clearances. Utilize the government’s BizPaL directory to identify municipal permits (such as food handling, personal services, or signage) mandated in your city.

Official sources: Ontario – Business Names Act Enforcement · Ontario Business Registry – Renew or Amend a Business Name
